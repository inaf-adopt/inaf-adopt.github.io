MATTO

A configurable laboratory test bench for developing and comparing multi-conjugate adaptive optics.

Overview

MATTO (Multi-conjugate Adaptive Techniques Test Optics) is a modular, wide-field adaptive-optics test bench being developed at INAF–Osservatorio Astronomico di Padova through the STILES programme. Rather than being a telescope instrument, it is intended as a controlled laboratory environment for assessing concepts relevant to multi-conjugate adaptive optics (MCAO) on large and extremely large telescopes.

Objectives

The facility is designed to compare wavefront-sensing, tomographic reconstruction and control approaches while varying the distribution of atmospheric turbulence and the geometry of guide stars. Its reconfigurable architecture is intended to support repeatable experiments, feasibility studies and validation of future MCAO techniques before they are considered for telescope deployment.

Architecture and methods

The published design separates MATTO into four configurable modules: sources, telescope/atmosphere simulation, MCAO correction and wavefront sensing. Up to three natural- and three laser-guide-star-like sources feed a simulator of telescope beam geometry and atmospheric perturbations. Three deformable mirrors, designed with approximately 800, 450 and 200 actuators, emulate correction at different turbulence heights. Pupil- and focal-plane sensor arrangements can be explored; DAO4MATTO provides the real-time control development framework based on Durham's DAO toolkit.

Development and status

Presentations in 2024 described the detailed design, while an April 2026 ADONI workshop contribution reported design and early implementation. Integration, alignment, calibration and real-time control are development activities, and the different simulated configurations still require experimental validation. The published material does not establish routine operation or verified on-sky performance; MATTO should therefore be described as a facility in development.

Our contribution

ADOPT group members contribute to the optical and opto-mechanical design, bench integration, wavefront-sensor configurations, calibration and real-time control development for MATTO, with the goal of validating new MCAO concepts in the laboratory.
