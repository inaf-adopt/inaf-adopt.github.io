MezzoCielo

A proposed monocentric telescope for continuous monitoring of a large fraction of the visible sky.

Overview

MezzoCielo (“half the sky”) is a proposed class of extremely wide-field, ground-based telescope designed to monitor a large fraction of the sky observable from one site simultaneously. Its spherical, monocentric optical system is intended to combine a comparatively large collecting aperture with unprecedented sky coverage. Published designs explore fields of roughly 5,000–20,000 square degrees, with around 10,000 square degrees often used as a reference goal. These figures describe proposed configurations, not measured on-sky performance.

Scientific objectives

The central scientific motivation is to observe the beginnings of short-lived astronomical events rather than discovering them hours or days later. Continuous, minute-cadence monitoring could reveal the early evolution of supernovae, kilonovae and gamma-ray bursts, and help identify electromagnetic counterparts to gravitational-wave or neutrino events. The ESO science white paper emphasises how this missing temporal coverage could complement classical sky surveys, including in the era of the Einstein Telescope. Further potential uses include space-debris and near-Earth-object tracking, stellar variability and exoplanet-transit monitoring.

Optical concept and engineering

The collector is conceived as a spherical envelope built from meniscus glass lenses arranged around a common optical centre, with a transparent, low-refractive-index optical liquid inside. Multiple correcting cameras and detectors sample different viewing directions and compensate for aberrations from the spherical primary optic. Research by Silvio Di Rosa, Roberto Ragazzoni, Demetrio Magrin and collaborators has investigated optical-fluid transmission and refractive-index variation, lens and frame loads, thermal effects, stress-induced birefringence, corrector optics and manufacturability. These studies test the feasibility of a segmented metre-class collector.

Development and status

MezzoCielo remains a development and prototyping programme, not an operational survey telescope. Papers published in 2026 describe progress from a 40 cm prototype towards a 1 m-class monocentric demonstrator and present further studies of the extremely wide-field optical design. The next challenges include validating collector and camera performance together, assembly and integration, thermal and mechanical stability, and high-cadence data handling. The longer-term goal is a continuously monitoring observatory able to identify transient phenomena very near their onset; its final capabilities and deployment remain to be demonstrated.
