EKARUS

A modular adaptive-optics R&D platform at Asiago, advancing on-sky testing, new technologies and training.

Context

EKARUS is a modular adaptive-optics (AO) research and development platform at the 1.82-m Copernico Telescope on Cima Ekar, Asiago, Italy. Developed through a collaboration led by INAF, LAM and ONERA, with international partners, it builds on the PAPYRUS testbed formerly operated at Observatoire de Haute-Provence. Its location at the telescope’s Coudé focus provides a dedicated optical bench for practical experimentation under real atmospheric conditions.

Objectives

Access to large observatories for experimental AO tests is limited. EKARUS offers an accessible, remotely operable environment to mature new wavefront sensors, control strategies and optical components before their possible use on larger telescopes. The modular layout is intended to support visiting experiments and, eventually, visible and near-infrared science applications. It also supports teaching: students can plan observations, calibrate the AO system, close the loop and examine the resulting images.

System and methods

EKARUS combines a pyramid wavefront sensor, high-speed detectors, a real-time controller and a deformable mirror with approximately 450 actuators. In closed loop, measurements of distorted stellar wavefronts drive mirror corrections at about 1,000 updates per second. The first stage reuses expertise and hardware from PAPYRUS, while the planned architecture includes a second-stage AO path and visitor ports. New sensing concepts, infrared capability and neural-network-based control remain development and testing goals rather than established routine operating modes.

First light and outlook

First light took place on 31 August and 1 September 2026, with stable closed-loop correction on bright stars including β Cassiopeiae. INAF reported a 10–20-fold improvement in image resolution under the observed conditions. The project team separately reported a Strehl ratio of about 40% at 650 nm in first-light testing, with seeing estimated at approximately 1.3 arcseconds at 500 nm. These are initial measurements, not guaranteed performance across all conditions. EKARUS is now a functioning AO test platform, with further calibration, new algorithms, an infrared channel and alternative wavefront sensors among its planned developments.

Our contribution

At INAF Padova, Davide Greggio is an EKARUS co-principal investigator and Alessandro Ballone leads work on the instrument-control software. Other ADOPT members contribute to AO modelling, opto-mechanical development, integration, calibration, commissioning and educational use in collaboration with LAM, ONERA and partner institutions.
