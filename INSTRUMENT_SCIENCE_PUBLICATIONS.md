# Science papers using commissioned instruments — ADOPT

Curated: **31 October 2026**. This list covers **published peer-reviewed astronomical science research using data taken with the named instrument**, rather than technical, commissioning, design, simulation or predicted science papers. It is a *selected* bibliography, not exhaustive. Publications involving multiple instruments explain the specific contribution. It does not imply the authors belong to ADOPT.

The separate section is rendered only when a project key has a nonempty list of verified papers in `instrument-science-papers.js`. All other projects have no science-results section—no empty placeholder is shown.

## SHARK-NIR

Peer-reviewed astrophysical research based on observations obtained with the commissioned SHARK-NIR instrument at the Large Binocular Telescope. Some studies combine its near-infrared data with observations from other facilities.

- Garreau et al. (2025), **[The HOSTS survey: Suspected variable dust emission and constraints on companions around θ Boo](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452653)**, Astronomy & Astrophysics 699, A107. DOI: `10.1051/0004-6361/202452653`. *SHARK-NIR H-band high-contrast observations, together with LMIRCam and LBTI measurements, constrain possible companions to θ Boötis and inform models of its circumstellar dust.*
- Barbato et al. (2025), **[A multi-technique detection of an eccentric giant planet around the accelerating star HD 57625](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452832)**, Astronomy & Astrophysics 693, A81. DOI: `10.1051/0004-6361/202452832`. *SHARK-NIR coronagraphic images placed limits on directly detectable companions. Combined radial velocities and astrometry revealed HD 57625 b; the planet itself was not directly imaged by SHARK-NIR.*
- Mesa et al. (2025), **[Deep imaging of three accelerating stars using SHARK-NIR and LMIRCam at LBT](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae2706)**, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 536, 1455–1466. DOI: `10.1093/mnras/stae2706`. *Uses SHARK-NIR H-band coronagraphic observations and complementary LMIRCam images to constrain possible companions around three accelerating nearby stars. Published online in December 2024; journal issue January 2025.*

## CHEOPS

Peer-reviewed exoplanet science based on photometric observations from the operational CHEOPS space telescope. Several studies also use complementary TESS or ground-based observations.

- Garai et al. (2026), **[CHEOPS photometry from 2024 reveals a reversal in the transit-timing variations of AU Mic c](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202659058)**, Astronomy & Astrophysics 713, A322. DOI: `10.1051/0004-6361/202659058`. *Repeated CHEOPS transit measurements of AU Mic b and c track planetary transit-timing variations and reveal a change in the trend for AU Mic c.*
- Bonfanti et al. (2026), **[The multi-planet system TOI-5624: four transiting sub-Neptunes with an outer companion revealed by transit-timing variations](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202558740)**, Astronomy & Astrophysics. DOI: `10.1051/0004-6361/202558740`. *CHEOPS and TESS transit photometry help establish four transiting planets and analyse timing variations indicating a further companion.*
- Lakeland et al. (2026), **[Discovery and characterization of two exoplanets orbiting the metal-poor, solar-type star TOI-5788 with TESS, CHEOPS, and HARPS-N](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag158)**, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 546. DOI: `10.1093/mnras/stag158`. *A dedicated CHEOPS observing programme, combined with TESS photometry and HARPS-N radial velocities, measures properties of two planets on opposite sides of the planetary radius gap.*
