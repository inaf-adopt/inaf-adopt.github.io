CHEOPS

High-precision exoplanet photometry with a space telescope whose optics were developed with INAF Padova.

Context

CHEOPS (CHaracterising ExOPlanet Satellite) is an ESA–Swiss space mission launched on 18 December 2019. It studies bright stars already known to host planets, rather than surveying the sky for entirely new systems. When a planet crosses its star, the small decrease in brightness reveals its size. Combined with an independently determined mass, the radius constrains the planet’s bulk density and possible internal structure. The mission focuses particularly on super-Earths and sub-Neptunes.

Scientific objectives

CHEOPS aims to establish precise planetary radii, investigate differences in composition and refine orbital properties. Repeated transits improve estimates of planetary periods and may reveal transit-timing variations in multiple-planet systems. The instrument can also observe occultations and phase variations of selected giant planets, providing information about reflected and emitted light. These measurements complement radial-velocity surveys and observations by other space and ground-based telescopes.

Optical design and AIV

The high-precision photometer uses a Ritchey–Chrétien telescope with a 320 mm entrance pupil (about 300 mm effective aperture), back-end reimaging optics and a CCD covering roughly 400–1100 nm. Stellar images are deliberately spread over multiple pixels to support stable photometry. Optical design, stray-light rejection, thermal stability and accurate calibration are crucial. Researchers from INAF Padova and Catania developed the optical design and supported industrial manufacture. A demonstration telescope at Padova was assembled, aligned and verified to validate methods later applied to the flight hardware.

Mission and results

The flight telescope was delivered to the Swiss-led CHEOPS consortium in 2017, and the spacecraft launched in December 2019. The mission remains operational. CHEOPS data have helped characterize the ultrahot planet WASP-189 b, reveal the resonant architecture of the six-planet TOI-178 system, and measure the striking reflectivity of LTT 9779 b. These are outcomes of the international mission collaboration. “Previous involvement” on the ADOPT website refers specifically to earlier group optical-development work, not the status of CHEOPS itself.

Our contribution

INAF Padova researchers worked on the telescope optical design, stray-light analysis, demonstration-model assembly and alignment, and ground-support equipment and verification procedures for the flight instrument, in collaboration with INAF Catania, the University of Bern and industrial partners.
