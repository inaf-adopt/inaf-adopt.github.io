SHARK-NIR

High-contrast near-infrared imaging and spectroscopy of exoplanets and circumstellar systems at the LBT.

Instrument publications ↓Science publications ↓

Context

SHARK-NIR (System for coronagraphy with High-order Adaptive optics from R to K band – Near Infrared) is a high-contrast camera and spectrograph at the Large Binocular Telescope (LBT) in Arizona. Led by INAF–Osservatorio Astronomico di Padova, the instrument observes between 0.96 and 1.70 µm and benefits from the LBT’s SOUL adaptive-optics system.

Objectives

SHARK-NIR aims to detect and characterize exoplanets and other faint companions close to bright stars. Its science programme also addresses circumstellar discs, jets and young stellar systems, alongside selected studies of Solar System objects, evolved stars and active galactic nuclei.

Methods

It combines classical and coronagraphic imaging, dual-band imaging and long-slit spectroscopy (R ≈ 100 or 700). Its coronagraphs include Gaussian Lyot, shaped-pupil and four-quadrant phase masks. Internal tip–tilt and deformable-mirror corrections reduce residual image motion and non-common-path aberrations. The instrument was assembled, aligned and verified at INAF Padova before installation at the LBT. High-contrast data can be analysed using angular differential imaging and principal-component analysis.

Results

SHARK-NIR obtained first light in January 2023. In initial commissioning observations, a contrast of about 5.9 × 10⁻⁶ at 1 arcsecond was measured for HD 49197 under the reported conditions. Later commissioning observations recovered all four known planets around HR 8799; the outer two were detected at signal-to-noise ratios above 20, and the inner two at about 6. Early-science studies have subsequently used SHARK-NIR images to constrain companions and investigate planetary systems. Imaging non-detections provide important limits but should not be mistaken for direct planet detections.

Science with the instrument

Selected refereed astrophysical studies using SHARK-NIR observations at the LBT, often combined with other instruments or techniques. Imaging non-detections can constrain unseen companions.

  1. The GAPS programme at TNG – LXX. HD128717 B/Gaia-6 B: A long-period eccentric low-mass brown dwarf from astrometry and radial velocities ↗
    Pinamonti et al. · 2026
    Astronomy & Astrophysics 707, A67

    SHARK-NIR H-band data constrain possible distant companions. The known brown dwarf was characterized using astrometry and radial velocities, not directly detected by SHARK-NIR.

  2. The HOSTS survey: Suspected variable dust emission and constraints on companions around θ Boo ↗
    Garreau et al. · 2025
    Astronomy & Astrophysics 699, A107

    SHARK-NIR H-band high-contrast observations, together with LMIRCam and LBTI measurements, constrain possible companions to θ Boötis and inform models of its circumstellar dust.

  3. A multi-technique detection of an eccentric giant planet around the accelerating star HD 57625 ↗
    Barbato et al. · 2025
    Astronomy & Astrophysics 693, A81

    SHARK-NIR coronagraphic images placed limits on directly detectable companions. Combined radial velocities and astrometry revealed HD 57625 b; the planet itself was not directly imaged by SHARK-NIR.

  4. Deep imaging of three accelerating stars using SHARK-NIR and LMIRCam at LBT ↗
    Mesa et al. · 2025
    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 536, 1455–1466

    Uses SHARK-NIR H-band coronagraphic observations and complementary LMIRCam images to constrain possible companions around three accelerating nearby stars. Published online in December 2024; journal issue January 2025.
