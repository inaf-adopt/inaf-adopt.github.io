SOXS

Rapid, simultaneous optical and near-infrared spectroscopy of transient events at the ESO NTT.

Context

SOXS (Son Of X-Shooter) is a medium-resolution, wide-band spectrograph at ESO’s 3.58-m New Technology Telescope (NTT) at La Silla, Chile. Built by an international consortium led by INAF, it has two complementary spectrograph arms and an acquisition and guiding camera. SOXS covers 350–2000 nm simultaneously, connecting the visible and near-infrared views of rapidly changing sources.

Objectives

The instrument provides prompt spectroscopic follow-up of alerts from wide-field surveys, telescopes and space missions. Its science programme includes supernovae, gamma-ray bursts, kilonovae, tidal disruption events and other transient or variable sources. Rapid observations and broad spectral coverage help classify events, measure redshifts and study their physical conditions while they are observable.

Methods

A common-path optical system feeds the UV–VIS and cryogenic near-infrared spectrographs, with resolving power typically around R ≈ 4,500, depending on wavelength and slit. Acquisition and guiding, wavelength and flat-field calibration, instrument control software, flexible scheduling and a data-reduction pipeline support efficient observations. End-to-end assembly, integration, verification and commissioning establish the instrument’s on-sky performance.

Results

SOXS was installed at the NTT in March 2025, followed by commissioning observations. ESO announced successful first observations in December 2025, including a 350–2000 nm spectrum of the supernova SN 2025advo recorded on 14 December 2025. User documentation and observing tools are available as the project transitions through commissioning towards regular scientific use. The illustrated supernova spectrum is a first-light demonstration, not a claim of a refereed discovery.
