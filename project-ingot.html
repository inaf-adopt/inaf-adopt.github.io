INGOT

A wavefront-sensor concept designed for the elongated sodium laser guide stars of extremely large telescopes.

Context

Laser guide stars (LGSs) make adaptive optics possible without a sufficiently bright natural star nearby. However, sodium beacons originate in an atmospheric layer roughly 90 km above the telescope and appear elongated when viewed from large apertures, especially with side-launched lasers. This creates difficulties for conventional wavefront sensors. Developed by researchers in Padova and collaborators, INGOT explores a pupil-plane approach whose optical geometry is matched to the beacon’s three-dimensional extent.

Objectives

The project investigates whether a sensor designed specifically for elongated LGSs can recover useful wavefront information more effectively and robustly than approaches adapted from point-source sensing. Key questions include sensitivity to atmospheric aberrations, the effects of varying sodium-layer brightness profiles, the accuracy of prism alignment and the suitability of different pupil configurations for ELT-class adaptive-optics systems. These are research objectives, not yet proven gains in on-sky telescope performance.

Methods

In the baseline three-pupil design, a reflective roof-shaped prism is placed near the focal volume of the elongated LGS. It separates light into three re-imaged telescope pupils, whose intensity distributions encode wavefront gradients; broader ingot concepts also consider six pupils. At INAF Padova, a laboratory bench simulates the beacon, uses precision positioning to align the prism and introduces known low-order aberrations with a deformable lens. Ray-tracing, end-to-end simulations, automatic optical-feedback alignment and variable sodium profiles support the measurements.

Results

Published studies established the optical concept and three- and six-pupil layouts and reported laboratory alignment and sensitivity tests. Work in 2020–2022 demonstrated methods to align the prototype and characterize its response to imposed low-order aberrations. The 2024 laboratory study examined improved alignment searches and time-varying sodium profiles; a separate 2024 journal article compared the possible sensing characteristics of ingot variants. Further closed-loop and on-sky validation is a development goal. INGOT is not an operational telescope wavefront sensor, and future performance advantages remain to be established.

Our contribution

Researchers at INAF Padova and collaborators have worked on the concept, optical design, numerical simulations, laboratory bench, opto-mechanical alignment, pupil-signal calibration and sensitivity tests. Recent work also studies sodium-layer profile variability and preparations for further validation.
