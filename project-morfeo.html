MORFEO

Multi-conjugate adaptive optics for sharp, uniform near-infrared imaging with MICADO on ESO’s ELT.

Overview

MORFEO (Multi-conjugate adaptive Optics Relay For ELT Observations) is a first-generation adaptive-optics module for the European Southern Observatory’s 39-metre Extremely Large Telescope (ELT). Coupled initially to the near-infrared camera MICADO, it is designed to deliver stable, high-resolution images over a 53 × 53 arcsecond field. MORFEO does not produce science observations itself: it corrects the telescope beam before it reaches the scientific instrument. A second output port is planned for future instrumentation.

Adaptive-optics concept

MORFEO combines measurements from six laser guide stars and three natural guide stars to reconstruct turbulence at different atmospheric heights. Two deformable mirrors inside the relay act together with the ELT’s adaptive M4 mirror, while the real-time control system coordinates the corrections. Natural stars provide low-order information such as tip, tilt and focus that cannot be fully measured from laser beacons. The system is being developed for multi-conjugate and single-conjugate adaptive-optics operation, with dedicated wavefront-sensing, calibration and alignment hardware.

Scientific objectives

With MICADO, MORFEO aims to resolve crowded stellar populations, measure stellar motions and positions precisely, and study star-forming regions in galaxies at redshifts around 2–3. Stable correction across the field will support astrometry and photometry, including investigations of stars near the Milky Way’s central black hole. ESO’s K-band (2.2 μm) targets are approximately 30% Strehl under median conditions and 50% in favourable conditions; these remain design requirements, not achieved on-sky results.

Development and status

As of 2026, the international MORFEO consortium is advancing towards its Final Design Review. Current work covers the deformable mirrors, laser-guide-star objective, wavefront sensors, real-time control, alignment tools and plans for manufacture, assembly, integration and verification. Published 2026 studies describe laboratory methods and design progress rather than completed telescope performance. The ADOPT group’s involvement includes optical engineering, verification and AIV planning, with particular attention to work on the ELT Nasmyth platform and coordination with laser-guide-star wavefront-sensor teams.

Our contribution

ADOPT members contribute to MORFEO optical design and opto-mechanical alignment, laboratory verification, and assembly, integration, verification and testing (AIVT) planning. Activities include developing alignment and metrology approaches, preparing work on the ELT Nasmyth platform, and coordinating interfaces with the laser-guide-star wavefront-sensor team. This work is carried out within the wider international MORFEO consortium.
