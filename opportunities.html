01 / STUDY

Master’s thesis projects

Master’s students in astronomy, physics, optics, engineering, computing and related disciplines may explore a research thesis connected to our instruments or laboratory activities. Possible projects range from numerical modelling to experimental optical work or astronomical data analysis.

Potential directions

  • Wavefront sensing and adaptive-optics simulations
  • Laboratory calibration, alignment and optical metrology
  • Instrument performance analysis and data reduction
  • Optical design, system modelling and verification

These are illustrative research directions, not pre-approved thesis titles. The topic, academic supervisor and assessment requirements must be agreed with the student's university.

02 / RESEARCH

PhD research

Doctoral research can investigate adaptive-optics concepts, laser and natural guide-star sensing, image reconstruction, astronomical instrumentation and the science enabled by these technologies. Topics may be developed with university and instrument-project collaborators.

Possible research themes

  • Multi-conjugate adaptive optics, wavefront sensing and control
  • Calibration, point-spread-function modelling and diagnostics
  • Instrument development, verification and commissioning
  • High-angular-resolution astronomical observations

PhD admission and funded positions are governed by the official university calls; discussing a research idea with ADOPT does not constitute an offer of admission or funding.

03 / EXPERIENCE

Internships & placements

Depending on the needs and capacity of current projects, a training placement might involve optical-bench experiments, software development, numerical simulations, systems engineering or astronomical data handling.

What to consider

  • Internships may be curricular or linked to a formal university training programme.
  • A placement requires suitable supervision and any necessary agreement, insurance and authorisation between the institutions.
  • Duration, eligibility, location and funding—if any—must be confirmed before arrangements are made.

Students from the University of Padova should consult its internship office and the requirements of their individual degree programme. Students from other universities should first confirm their home institution's procedures.

GET IN TOUCH

How to enquire

Find a relevant group member using the People directory, then contact them through an appropriate institutional channel. Briefly describe your interests and mention the type of opportunity you are seeking.

  • Your degree programme, university and current stage of study
  • The research area or project that interests you
  • Your approximate start date and available duration
  • A short CV and any academic or internship requirements

Informal enquiries do not replace official applications. Please do not send sensitive personal documents until requested through an authorised process.
