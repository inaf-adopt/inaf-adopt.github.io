LINC-NIRVANA

Layer-oriented multi-conjugate adaptive optics and near-infrared imaging at the Large Binocular Telescope.

Context

LINC-NIRVANA is a German–Italian near-infrared imager developed for the Large Binocular Telescope (LBT), whose two primary mirrors each measure 8.4 metres across. Installed at the telescope in 2016, it was conceived to combine multi-conjugate adaptive optics (MCAO) with eventual interferometric imaging. Its original Fizeau beam-combination concept aimed to exploit the LBT’s approximately 23-metre maximum baseline; this was a design objective rather than an established routine science mode.

Objectives

The project sought sharp near-infrared images for studies of crowded stellar populations, star-forming regions, circumstellar environments and compact structures. Its innovative approach uses natural guide stars in two fields to measure turbulence at different atmospheric heights. The MCAO sensing and correction concept spans a two-arcminute field, while the original infrared science-camera field is much smaller, approximately 10 × 10 arcseconds; these two fields should not be confused.

Methods

Each LBT arm has ground-layer and high-layer pyramid wavefront sensors. The ground-layer system can combine light from up to 12 natural guide stars in an annulus around the science field, driving the telescope’s 672-actuator adaptive secondary mirror. The high-layer sensor can use up to eight guide stars within the central two-arcminute field and drive a 349-actuator deformable mirror conjugated to about 7.1 km altitude. Commissioning demanded precise alignment of the large optical bench, multiple star probes, telescope interfaces and changing flexure.

Results and legacy

First technical light was recorded in November 2016 and MCAO first light was reported in April 2018. Later on-sky ground-layer adaptive-optics tests demonstrated point-spread-function width improvements of up to a factor of three under the reported conditions. The project produced important experience in multi-pyramid wavefront sensing, alignment, calibration and commissioning. Its envisioned interferometric capability should not be presented as a routinely achieved observing mode, and LINC-NIRVANA is no longer in regular scientific use. Its existing platform also motivates the separately developed NIRVANA-VIS visible-channel upgrade.

Our contribution

ADOPT group members contributed to the ground-layer pyramid wavefront sensors and star-probe systems, opto-mechanical alignment, integration, calibration and on-sky MCAO commissioning. These activities involved close collaboration among INAF, MPIA and the LBT Observatory.
