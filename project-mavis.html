MAVIS

Wide-field, high-resolution visible imaging and integral-field spectroscopy for ESO's Very Large Telescope.

Context

MAVIS (MCAO-Assisted Visible Imager and Spectrograph) is a next-generation instrument being developed for ESO’s Very Large Telescope (VLT) at Paranal Observatory in Chile. Led by an Australian–European consortium, it will be mounted at the Nasmyth A focus of Unit Telescope 4 (Yepun). MAVIS is designed to bring high-angular-resolution adaptive-optics observations into the visible spectrum, complementing infrared instruments on the VLT and the Extremely Large Telescope.

Scientific objectives

MAVIS aims to resolve crowded star fields, map individual stars and study stellar populations and their chemical evolution. Its imager and integral-field spectrograph will also probe star formation and dynamics in nearby and distant galaxies, the formation of early galaxies, and compact objects. Planned Solar System applications include atmospheric structure on planets and moons. These are future science capabilities, not results obtained with MAVIS.

Adaptive-optics system

The Adaptive Optics Module (AOM) combines the VLT Adaptive Optics Facility’s deformable secondary mirror with two post-focal deformable mirrors. Up to eight laser guide stars and three natural guide stars support tomographic reconstruction of atmospheric turbulence over a 30 × 30 arcsec corrected field. The optical relay also includes field de-rotation, atmospheric dispersion correction and calibration subsystems. A wide-field visible imager and an integral-field spectrograph receive the corrected light, targeting angular resolution down to approximately 18 milliarcseconds.

Development and status

MAVIS completed its Preliminary Design Review in March 2023 and held its Final Design Review at ESO in October 2025. Development continues through engineering, subsystem prototyping, manufacturing preparation, calibration planning and system verification. ESO currently lists first light in 2031. The published field, sensitivity and resolution specifications are design goals rather than demonstrated on-sky performance.

Our contribution

ADOPT researchers contribute to the MAVIS Adaptive Optics Module through optical design and tolerance analysis, calibration and non-common-path-aberration strategies, opto-mechanical design and prototyping, system engineering, integration planning and verification, in collaboration with the international MAVIS consortium.
